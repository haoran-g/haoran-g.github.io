PPIMCE
PPIMCE: An In-Memory Computing Fabric for Privacy-Preserving Computing
Published in: Journal of Computer Science and Technology (JCST), 2025
Authors: Haoran Geng, Jianqiao Mo, Dayane Reis, Jonathan Takeshita, Taeho Jung, Brandon Reagen, Michael Niemier, Xiaobo Sharon Hu
Background

Privacy-preserving machine learning (PPML) requires computing on encrypted data, which is 10,000×–100,000× slower than plaintext on GPUs. The dominant bottleneck in HE-only protocols is bootstrapping, which consumes over 95% of computation time. Combining Homomorphic Encryption (HE) for linear layers with Garbled Circuits (GC) for non-linear layers eliminates bootstrapping, but existing hardware accelerators target either HE or GC individually — not both. Adding a GC accelerator alongside a state-of-the-art HE ASIC like CraterLake (472 mm², 280W at 7nm) would be prohibitive in area and power, while inter-accelerator data movement would add further latency.

Design

PPIMCE extends the IMCRYPTO in-SRAM computing architecture to a multi-core system that natively supports both HE and GC in a unified platform. The architecture consists of an IMC-Instruction Scheduler (IMC-IS) with a CAM-based data dependency checker and N IMC cores, each containing Compute-Enabled Memory (CEM) arrays, a LUT fabric for AES SubBytes/MixColumns and 4-bit integer multiplication, a bidirectional shifter, and a core controller with a micro-instruction memory. HE polynomial operations exploit SIMD parallelism across all cores; GC Half-Gate computations are parallelized across 16 GC Computing Units.

PPIMCE achieves 107× speedup for GC benchmarks over CPU with 16 cores. For HE, it delivers 1500×–5000× speedup for addition, 100×–1500× for multiplication, and 110×–2000× for rotation vs. CPU, and up to 800× vs. GPU. For end-to-end PPML inference, PPIMCE is 1000× faster than Gazelle and achieves 12× speedup over CraterLake, with 91.9% accuracy on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-20) and 76.1% on ImageNet (ResNet-50).

Conclusion

PPIMCE demonstrates that a single, compact in-SRAM computing fabric can replace the combination of a large HE ASIC and a separate GC accelerator, achieving superior performance with dramatically lower area and power. The unified design eliminates inter-accelerator data movement overhead that would otherwise dominate PPML inference latency.
