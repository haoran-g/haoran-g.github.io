EvaX-Poly
Towards Noise-Aware and Efficient Homomorphic Encryption on Crossbar Arrays
Venue: Under Review — IEEE Transactions on Computer-Aided Design (TCAD)
Authors: Haoran Geng, Shaocong Wang, Mengyuan Li, Tomas Sousa Pereira, Michael Niemier, Xiaobo Sharon Hu
Background

Crossbar arrays (XBAs) with ReRAM devices are promising accelerators for Polynomial Modular Multiplication (PMM) in CKKS-based HE, but they are an inherently lossy computing fabric. Device cycle-to-cycle conductance variations and ADC quantization errors introduce noise into every VMM computation. Crucially, CKKS encryption also intentionally adds noise as a security parameter — noise which accumulates through multiplications. No prior work had systematically studied whether XBA-induced noise is compatible with HE correctness requirements, or how to determine the maximum tolerable device noise level for a given HE configuration.

Design

EvaX-Poly introduces an analytical noise evaluation model with three components: (1) derivation of SNRHE_min — the minimum SNR required for correct CKKS decryption as a function of polynomial degree N, ciphertext modulus q, and multiplicative depth; (2) modeling of hardware-induced XBA noise, including read noise (measured from a real 40 nm ReRAM array with 3000 repeated reads per cell across 8 conductance states) and ADC quantization noise, both modeled as zero-mean Gaussians; (3) analysis determining whether SNRCB ≥ SNRHE_min, and strategies (MSB/LSB bit-precision assignment) when the condition is not met.

For 40 nm ReRAM devices (θ ≈ 0.05 at 31 µS), EvaX-Poly shows that CKKS operations at multiplicative depth 1–2 are feasible without any noise mitigation — covering federated learning and secure inference protocols (Gazelle, Delphi). The SNR crossover point occurs at θ ≈ 1.21×10⁻¹, with SNRCB degrading rapidly beyond this threshold. Multi-bit conductance mappings with low-noise state preference can extend viable depth.

Conclusion

EvaX-Poly provides the first rigorous framework for determining whether a given XBA implementation can correctly execute HE workloads, closing the gap between device-level ReRAM characterization and cryptographic correctness requirements. Current 40 nm ReRAM devices already support practical CKKS workloads, with room for improvement as device technology advances.
